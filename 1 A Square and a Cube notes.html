1.1 Square Numbers & Properties[cite: 2]

A perfect square is formed by multiplying a natural number by itself[cite: 2]. Geometrically, it represents the area of a square[cite: 2].

General Notation: \( n \times n = n^2 \)[cite: 2]
Examples: \( 1^2 = 1, 2^2 = 4, 3^2 = 9, 4^2 = 16 \)[cite: 2]

Key Properties[cite: 2]:

  • Ending Digits: All perfect squares end with 0, 1, 4, 5, 6, or 9[cite: 2]. They never end in 2, 3, 7, or 8[cite: 2].
  • Number of Zeros: The number of zeros at the end of a square is double the number of zeros at the end of its base number[cite: 2]. Squares can only have an even number of zeros[cite: 2].
  • Parity: The square of an even number is even, and the square of an odd number is odd[cite: 2].
Sum of Odd Numbers: Every square can be expressed as a sum of successive odd numbers starting from 1[cite: 2].
Example: \( 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 = 25 = 5^2 \)[cite: 2]

1.2 Cubic Numbers[cite: 2]

A number obtained by multiplying a number by itself three times is called a cube[cite: 2].

General Notation: \( n^3 = n \times n \times n \)[cite: 2]
Examples: \( 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, \dots \)[cite: 2]

Taxicab Numbers: Numbers that can be expressed as the sum of two cubes in two different ways. The most famous is the Hardy-Ramanujan Number: 1729[cite: 2].

\( 1729 = 1^3 + 12^3 = 9^3 + 10^3 \)[cite: 2]

Common Mistakes to Avoid

⚠️ Factoring Assumptions

  • Zeros Rule: A perfect cube cannot end with exactly two zeroes (00)[cite: 2].
  • Factor Parity: Cube numbers do NOT necessarily have an odd number of factors. Square numbers have an odd number of factors[cite: 2].

Square Roots & Cube Roots[cite: 2]

Square root is the inverse operation of a square, denoted by \( \sqrt{} \)[cite: 2]. A number is a perfect square if its prime factors can be split into two identical groups[cite: 2].

Similarly, the cube root is denoted by \( \sqrt[3]{} \)[cite: 2]. A number is a perfect cube if its prime factors can be split into three identical groups[cite: 2].

Page 16-17: Figure it Out[cite: 2]

Q1. Find the cube roots of 27000 and 10648[cite: 2].
Solution:
Use prime factorization to group factors into triplets[cite: 2].
Final Answer: Cube root of 27000 is 30, and the cube root of 10648 is 22[cite: 2].
Q2. What number will you multiply by 1323 to make it a cube number?[cite: 2]
Solution:
Factorizing 1323 yields \( 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 7 \times 7 \). The factor 7 is not part of a complete triplet (it needs one more 7)[cite: 2].
Final Answer: 7[cite: 2]
Q3. State true or false: (i) The cube of any odd number is even. (ii) There is no perfect cube that ends with 8[cite: 2].
Solution:
(i) Odd numbers multiplied three times result in odd products. (ii) \( 2^3 = 8 \), so perfect cubes can end in 8[cite: 2].
Final Answer: (i) False (ii) False[cite: 2].
Q5. Which of the following is the greatest? (i) \( 67^3 - 66^3 \) (ii) \( 43^3 - 42^3 \) (iii) \( 67^2 - 66^2 \) (iv) \( 43^2 - 42^2 \)[cite: 2]
Solution:
Evaluate the differences between the values. The differences between successive cubes grow much faster than successive squares[cite: 2].
Final Answer: (i) \( 67^3 - 66^3 \) is the greatest[cite: 2].
