A Story of Numbers

Evolution of Numerals and Base Systems

3.1 – 3.3: Early Number Systems and the Idea of a Base

Humans have needed to count since the Stone Age to track food, livestock, and passing days. Early methods included one-to-one mapping using physical objects (like sticks or pebbles) or tally marks on bones (e.g., Ishango and Lebombo bones).

Gumulgal System: The indigenous Gumulgal people in Australia formed number names by counting in groups of 2. For instance, 3 was "ukasar-urapon" (\(2 + 1\)) and 4 was "ukasar-ukasar" (\(2 + 2\)).
Base-n Number System
A number system where the first landmark number is 1, and every next landmark number is obtained by multiplying the current one by a fixed number n.
Landmark numbers of a base-n system: \(n^0, n^1, n^2, n^3\dots\)

The Egyptian System: This was a base-10 system that used unique symbols for 1, 10, 100, 1,000, up to 10,000,000. It used grouping but lacked place value.

Q. Compute the landmark numbers of a base-7 system. In general, what are the landmark numbers of a base-n system?
Solution:
Step 1: In a base-7 system, the landmark numbers are powers of 7.
Step 2: Calculate the powers: \(7^0 = 1, 7^1 = 7, 7^2 = 49, 7^3 = 343\), and so on.
Final Answer: The landmark numbers are 1, 7, 49, 343... For a base-n system, they are \(n^0, n^1, n^2, n^3\dots\)

3.4: Place Value Representation & World Systems

As civilizations evolved, so did the efficiency of their number representations, leading to the breakthrough of place-value systems.

  • Roman Numerals: Uses landmark numbers I (1), V (5), X (10), L (50), C (100), D (500), M (1000). Arithmetic calculations (multiplication/division) were notoriously difficult in this system.
  • Mesopotamian (Babylonian) System: A Base-60 (sexagesimal) system. It introduced positional placement (place value) to distinguish multiples of 60, though early versions lacked a true zero, creating spacing ambiguities.
  • Mayan System: A Base-20 system written vertically. Importantly, it included a shell symbol for zero as a placeholder.
  • Chinese System: A Base-10 (decimal) rod numeral system that alternated vertical (Zong) and horizontal (Heng) rods to clarify place values.
Common Student Error:
Misinterpreting the Chinese Zong/Heng alternating system. Without alternating rod directions, a number like 41 could be misread as 23, 32, or 122 if spacing wasn't perfectly clear.

Q. Represent the following numbers in the Roman system: (i) 1222 (ii) 2999 (iii) 302 (iv) 715
Solution:
Step 1: Break down 1222 into landmark numbers: 1000 + 100 + 100 + 10 + 10 + 1 + 1 → MCCXXII
Step 2: Break down 2999: 1000 + 1000 + 900(CM) + 90(XC) + 9(IX) → MMCMXCIX
Step 3: Break down 302: 100 + 100 + 100 + 1 + 1 → CCCII
Step 4: Break down 715: 500 + 100 + 100 + 10 + 5 → DCCXV
Final Answers:
(i) MCCXXII
(ii) MMCMXCIX
(iii) CCCII
(iv) DCCXV

The Hindu Number System & Final Exercises

The Hindu Number System is the foundation of modern mathematics. Originating in India around 2000 years ago, it is a Base-10 (decimal) place-value system utilizing ten symbols (0-9).

The Power of Zero
Zero (0) acts not only as a positional placeholder (to distinguish 35 from 305) but also as a distinct mathematical number capable of arithmetic operations (codified by Brahmagupta).

Figure it Out (Page 80)

Q. Form a base-2 place value system using 'ukasar' and 'urapon' as the digits. Compare this system with that of the Gumulgal's.
Solution:
Step 1: In Base-2, we need symbols for 0 and 1. Assign 0 = urapon (ur) and 1 = ukasar (uk).
Step 2: Compare representations. For number 1: Base-2 is 'uk' (\(1 \times 2^0\)). Gumulgal is 'ur'.
Step 3: Number 4: Base-2 is 'uk ur ur' (\(1 \times 2^2 + 0 \times 2^1 + 0 \times 2^0\)). Gumulgal is 'uk-uk'.
Step 4: Number 5: Base-2 is 'uk ur uk' (\(1 \times 2^2 + 0 \times 2^1 + 1 \times 2^0\)). Gumulgal is 'uk-uk-ur'.
Final Answer: The Base-2 system utilizes positional place value (powers of 2), whereas the Gumulgal system strictly groups by 2s additively without positional value. Both only use two primary identifiers.
Q. Try writing the base-10 numeral 25 as base-8 and base-5 numerals, respectively.
Solution:
Step 1: For Base-8, find the highest power of 8 that fits in 25. \(8^1 = 8\). \(25 \div 8 = 3\) with remainder 1. So, \((3 \times 8^1) + (1 \times 8^0)\).
Step 2: Base-8 representation is 31.
Step 3: For Base-5, find the highest power of 5. \(5^2 = 25\). \(25 \div 25 = 1\) with remainder 0. So, \((1 \times 5^2) + (0 \times 5^1) + (0 \times 5^0)\).
Step 4: Base-5 representation is 100.
Final Answer: Base-8 representation of 25 is 31. Base-5 representation of 25 is 100.
