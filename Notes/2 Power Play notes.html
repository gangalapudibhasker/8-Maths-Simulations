Exponential Growth, Scientific Notation, and Operations

2.1 Experiencing the Power Play & 2.2 Exponential Notation

Multiplicative growth, also called exponential growth, is extremely rapid compared to linear (additive) growth. For example, folding a piece of paper doubles its thickness with each fold, leading to astronomical numbers quickly.

Exponential Notation
\( n^a \) denotes \( n \) multiplied by itself \( a \) times.
Here, \( a \) is the exponent/power, and \( n \) is the base.

Square numbers can be expressed as \( n^2 \) ('n squared') and cube numbers as \( n^3 \) ('n cubed'). A number can be broken down into its prime factors and written in exponential form.

Note: Remember that \( 4 + 4 + 4 = 3 \times 4 = 12 \), whereas \( 4 \times 4 \times 4 = 4^3 = 64 \).

Figure it Out (Page 22-23)

Q1. Express the following in exponential form: (i) \( 6 \times 6 \times 6 \times 6 \) (iv) \( 5 \times 5 \times 7 \times 7 \times 7 \)
Solution:
Step 1: Count how many times each base number is multiplied by itself.
Step 2: For (i), 6 is multiplied 4 times. For (iv), 5 is multiplied 2 times and 7 is multiplied 3 times.
Final Answer: (i) \( 6^4 \)
(iv) \( 5^2 \times 7^3 \)
Q2. Express 3600 as a product of powers of their prime factors in exponential form.
Solution:
Step 1: Perform prime factorization on 3600. \( 3600 = 2 \times 1800 = 2 \times 2 \times 900 \) etc.
Step 2: Continue factoring: \( 3600 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5 \).
Step 3: Group the identical prime factors and write them using exponents.
Final Answer: \( 2^4 \times 3^2 \times 5^2 \)
Q3. Write the numerical value of: \( (-3)^2 \times (-5)^2 \)
Solution:
Step 1: Evaluate each term. \( (-3)^2 = (-3) \times (-3) = 9 \).
Step 2: \( (-5)^2 = (-5) \times (-5) = 25 \).
Step 3: Multiply the results: \( 9 \times 25 \).
Final Answer: 225

2.3 The Other Side of Powers & 2.4 Powers of 10

Operations with exponents follow specific generalized rules that make calculations involving very large or very small numbers much easier.

Laws of Exponents
\( n^a \times n^b = n^{a+b} \)
\( (n^a)^b = (n^b)^a = n^{ab} \)
\( n^a \div n^b = n^{a-b} \) (where \( n \neq 0 \))
\( n^a \times m^a = (nm)^a \)
\( n^0 = 1 \) (where \( n \neq 0 \))
\( n^{-a} = \frac{1}{n^a} \)
Common Student Error:
Confusing \( n^0 \) with \( 0 \). Remember that any non-zero number raised to the power of 0 is exactly 1. For example, \( 2^0 = 1 \), not \( 0 \).

Scientific Notation: In standard form, we write numbers as \( x \times 10^y \), where \( x \ge 1 \) and \( x < 10 \), and \( y \) is an integer. The exponent \( y \) is critical as it indicates the scale (number of digits) of the value.

Figure it Out (Page 29 & 32)

Q1. Simplify and write in exponential form: \( 3^2 \times 3^{-5} \times 3^6 \)
Solution:
Step 1: Apply the multiplication rule for exponents with the same base: \( n^a \times n^b \times n^c = n^{a+b+c} \).
Step 2: Add the exponents: \( 2 + (-5) + 6 \).
Step 3: \( 2 - 5 + 6 = 3 \).
Final Answer: \( 3^3 \)
Q2. Express the following numbers in standard form: (i) 59,853 (iv) 70,04,00,00,000
Solution:
Step 1: Place a decimal point after the first non-zero digit to create a coefficient between 1 and 10.
Step 2: Count the number of places the decimal point moved to find the exponent for 10.
Step 3: For 59,853, the decimal moves 4 places left: \( 5.9853 \times 10^4 \).
Step 4: For 70,04,00,00,000, the decimal moves 10 places left: \( 7.004 \times 10^{10} \).
Final Answer: (i) \( 5.9853 \times 10^4 \)
(iv) \( 7.004 \times 10^{10} \)

2.5 Did You Ever Wonder? & Final Exercises

We can estimate real-world phenomena using powers of 10 to grasp incredibly large values. Engaging in thought experiments helps us understand how large a number or quantity truly is.

  • 1 lakh is \( 10^5 \)
  • 1 crore is \( 10^7 \)
  • 1 million is \( 10^6 \)
  • 1 billion is \( 10^9 \)
Chapter Summary Highlights:
* Multiplicative growth (\( n^a \)) escalates much faster than additive growth.
* Large numbers like \( 308,100,000 \) are succinctly written as \( 3.081 \times 10^8 \).
* The laws of exponents are vital for simplifying complex multiplicative chains.

Final Exercises (Pages 44 - 46)

Q1. Find out the units digit in the value of \( 2^{224} \div 4^{32} \)? [Hint: \( 4 = 2^2 \)]
Solution:
Step 1: Convert \( 4^{32} \) to base 2. \( 4^{32} = (2^2)^{32} = 2^{64} \).
Step 2: Perform the division using exponent rules: \( 2^{224} \div 2^{64} = 2^{224 - 64} = 2^{160} \).
Step 3: Find the unit digit of \( 2^{160} \). The unit digit of powers of 2 repeats in a cycle of 4 (2, 4, 8, 6).
Step 4: Since 160 is perfectly divisible by 4, the unit digit corresponds to the 4th position in the cycle, which is 6.
Final Answer: The units digit is '6'.
Q3. Write the given number as the product of two or more powers in three different ways: (i) \( 64^3 \)
Solution:
Step 1: First, write 64 in terms of prime bases: \( 64 = 2^6 \). So \( 64^3 = (2^6)^3 = 2^{18} \).
Step 2: Way 1 - Split the exponent: \( 2^{10} \times 2^8 = 2^{18} \).
Step 3: Way 2 - Use base 4: \( 64 = 4^3 \). \( (4^3)^3 = 4^9 = 4^5 \times 4^4 \).
Step 4: Way 3 - Use base 8: \( 64 = 8^2 \). \( (8^2)^3 = 8^6 = 8^3 \times 8^3 \).
Final Answer: Some of the ways are \( 2^{10} \times 2^8 \), \( 4^5 \times 4^4 \), \( 8^3 \times 8^3 \).
Q8. Identify the greater number: \( 4^3 \) or \( 3^4 \)
Solution:
Step 1: Evaluate \( 4^3 = 4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64 \).
Step 2: Evaluate \( 3^4 = 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 81 \).
Step 3: Compare 64 and 81.
Final Answer: \( 3^4 \) is greater than \( 4^3 \).
Q14. What was the date 1 billion seconds ago?
Solution:
Step 1: A billion seconds is \( 1 \times 10^9 \) seconds.
Step 2: Convert to days: \( 10^9 \div (24 \times 60 \times 60) \approx 11574 \) days.
Step 3: Convert days to years: \( 11574 \div 365.25 \approx 31.7 \) years ago.
Step 4: Subtract 31.7 years from the present date. Today is August 2026. Therefore, 1 billion seconds ago was approximately December 1994.
Final Answer: Approximately 31.7 years ago (which is around late 1994).
