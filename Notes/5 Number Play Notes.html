5.1 Is This a Multiple Of?

When selecting 4 consecutive numbers and placing '+' and '-' signs between them in any combination, the resulting mathematical expressions will always evaluate to an even number.

  • All combinations of \(a \pm b \pm c \pm d\) will invariably share the same parity.
  • If the difference between two numbers is even, they cannot have different parities (meaning both are even or both are odd).
General Properties of Divisibility
If \(A\) is divisible by \(k\), then all multiples of \(A\) are divisible by \(k\).
If \(A\) is divisible by \(k\), then \(A\) is divisible by all the factors of \(k\).
If \(a\) divides \(m\) and \(a\) divides \(n\), then \(a\) divides \(m+n\) and \(m-n\).
Note: Numbers that leave a remainder of 3 when divided by 5 take the algebraic form \(5k+3\). Alternatively, they can be interpreted as 2 less than a multiple of 5, written as \(5k-2\).

Figure it Out (Page 122)

Q1. The sum of four consecutive numbers is 34. What are these numbers?
Solution:
Step 1: Formulate the algebraic equation. Let the four consecutive numbers be \(x, x+1, x+2\), and \(x+3\).
Step 2: Calculate the sum: \(4x + 6 = 34\).
Step 3: Solve for \(x\): \(4x = 28\), which means \(x = 7\).
Final Answer: The required consecutive numbers are 7, 8, 9 and 10.
Q2. Suppose \(p\) is the greatest of five consecutive numbers. Describe the other four numbers in terms of \(p\).
Solution:
Step 1: Recognize that consecutive integers decrease sequentially by exactly 1.
Step 2: Since \(p\) is the greatest number, subtract 1 iteratively to find the preceding numbers.
Final Answer: The other four are \(p-1, p-2, p-3\) and \(p-4\).

5.2 Checking Divisibility Quickly

We can establish robust rules for checking divisibility without executing full division calculations, relying on algebraic structures instead.

Divisibility by 9
A number is divisible by 9 if and only if the sum of its digits is divisible by 9.
Divisibility by 11
Compute the difference between the sum of digits at odd places and even places. If this difference is 11, a multiple of 11, or 0, the number is divisible by 11.

Digital Roots: To find a number's digital root, repeatedly add its digits until a single-digit number is obtained. For instance, the digital root of multiples of 9 will always be 9.

Common Student Error:
Assuming that checking divisibility by any two factors guarantees divisibility by their product. For example, testing divisibility by 24 simply by verifying divisibility by 4 and by 6.

Correction: This does not work because 4 and 6 share a common factor (2). For example, 12 is divisible by both 4 and 6, but it is not divisible by 24. To check divisibility by 24 correctly, use co-prime factors like 3 and 8.

Figure it Out (Page 126 & 128)

Q1. Find, without dividing, whether the following numbers are divisible by 9: (i) 123 (ii) 405 (iii) 8888 (iv) 93547 (v) 358095
Solution:
Step 1: Calculate the sum of the digits for each number.
Step 2: For 405, the sum is \(4+0+5 = 9\), which is divisible by 9. The other numbers do not yield a sum divisible by 9.
Final Answer: Only the number in (ii) 405 is divisible by 9.
Q2. Find the smallest multiple of 9 with no odd digits.
Solution:
Step 1: Identify that the available even digits are 2, 4, 6, 8.
Step 2: The sum of digits must be a multiple of 9. A sum of 9 is impossible using only even numbers, so the smallest possible target sum is 18.
Step 3: The optimal combination of even digits that sums to 18 is (2, 8, 8). To form the smallest number, arrange them in ascending order.
Final Answer: Arranging these digits yields 288, which is the smallest number divisible by 9 with no odd digits.

5.3 Digits in Disguise & Chapter Summary

Cryptarithms: These are mathematical puzzles where each letter stands for a distinct digit, and each digit is represented by at most one letter. A critical rule is that the first digit of a number is never 0. Solving these puzzles requires logical reasoning, recognizing patterns, and applying operational properties.

Chapter Summary Highlights:
* If a number \(a\) is divisible by \(b\) and is also divisible by \(c\), then \(a\) must be divisible by the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of \(b\) and \(c\).
* Mathematical thinking fundamentally involves utilizing algebra, visualization, examples, and counterexamples to prove statements.

Final Exercises (Page 131 - 134)

Q1. The digital root of an 8-digit number is 5. What will be the digital root of 10 more than that number?
Solution:
Step 1: Adding 10 to a number effectively increases its digit sum by 1 (because \(1+0=1\)).
Step 2: The new digital root will be the old root plus 1.
Final Answer: The new digital root is \(5+1=6\).
Q2. If 31z5 is a multiple of 9, where z is a digit, what is the value of z?
Solution:
Step 1: For a number to be a multiple of 9, the sum of its digits must be a multiple of 9.
Step 2: Construct the equation: \(3+1+z+5 = 9+z\).
Step 3: For \(9+z\) to be a multiple of 9 (like 9 or 18), \(z\) must evaluate correctly as a single digit.
Final Answer: So, \(z=0\) or 9.
Q3. Find three consecutive numbers such that the first number is a multiple of 2, the second number is a multiple of 3, and the third number is a multiple of 4.
Solution:
Step 1: Let three consecutive numbers be represented as \(n, n+1\), and \(n+2\).
Step 2: Here, \(n\) is a multiple of 2, \((n+1)\) is a multiple of 3, and \((n+2)\) is a multiple of 4.
Step 3: Testing small numbers yields the set 2, 3, 4. To find the next sequence, utilize the LCM of 2, 3, and 4, which is 12. The pattern repeats every 12.
Final Answer: One set is 2, 3, 4. The next set of 3 consecutive numbers is 14, 15 and 16.
