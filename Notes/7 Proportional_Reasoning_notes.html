6.1 Some Properties of Multiplication

The distributive property helps us understand increments in products and gives us a way to easily calculate them[cite: 22]. It provides a bridge between multiplication and addition.

Distributive Property of Multiplication over Addition
\(a(b + c) = ab + ac\)

We can extend this idea to see how products change when BOTH numbers in a multiplication are increased[cite: 22].

Identity 1
\((a + m)(b + n) = ab + mb + an + mn\)
Note: This identity can be visualized geometrically. The product \((a+m)(b+n)\) is the sum of the products of each term[cite: 22].

Figure it Out (Page 142)

Q1. Expand the product: \((3 + u)(v - 3)\)
Solution:
Step 1: Treat this as Identity 1 where the terms are 3, \(u\) and \(v\), \(-3\).
Step 2: Apply distributive property: \(3(v - 3) + u(v - 3)\)
Step 3: Expand the terms: \(3v - 9 + uv - 3u\)[cite: 22]
Final Answer: \(3v - 3u + uv - 9\)[cite: 22]
Q2. Use the fast multiplication rule to evaluate: \(94 \times 11\)
Solution:
Step 1: Write 11 as \((10 + 1)\)[cite: 22].
Step 2: \(94 \times (10 + 1) = 940 + 94\)[cite: 22].
Step 3: Add the values: \(9 + 1\) (carry over) = 10, \(4 + 9 = 13\) (so 3 with 1 carried), \(4 + 0 = 4\).
Final Answer: 1034[cite: 22]
Q3. Evaluate: \(265831 \times 1001\)
Solution:
Step 1: Express 1001 as \((1000 + 1)\).
Step 2: \(265831 \times (1000 + 1) = 265831000 + 265831\)[cite: 22].
Final Answer: 266096831[cite: 22]

6.2 Special Cases of the Distributive Property

When applying the distributive property to multiply identical binomials, or binomials with the same terms but opposite signs, we arrive at standard algebraic identities[cite: 22].

Identity 1A (Square of a Sum)
\((a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2\)
Identity 1B (Square of a Difference)
\((a - b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\)
Identity 1C (Difference of Squares)
\((a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2\)

Figure it Out (Page 149)

Q1. Which is greater: \((a - b)^2\) or \((b - a)^2\)? Justify your answer.
Solution:
Step 1: Expand \((a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2\)[cite: 22].
Step 2: Expand \((b - a)^2 = b^2 - 2ab + a^2\).
Step 3: Compare the terms. Due to the commutative property of addition, the expressions are identical.
Final Answer: Both are equal[cite: 22].
Q2. Express 100 as the difference of two squares.
Solution:
Step 1: We are looking for \(a^2 - b^2 = 100\)[cite: 22].
Step 2: Test square numbers that have a difference of 100[cite: 22].
Step 3: Notice that 676 (which is \(26^2\)) and 576 (which is \(24^2\)) yield a difference of 100[cite: 22].
Final Answer: \(26^2 - 24^2 = 100\)[cite: 22]
Q3. Find \(406^2\) using the identities you have learnt.
Solution:
Step 1: Write 406 as \((400 + 6)\).
Step 2: Apply Identity 1A: \((400 + 6)^2 = 400^2 + 2(400)(6) + 6^2\).
Step 3: Calculate: \(160000 + 4800 + 36\).
Final Answer: 164836[cite: 22]

6.3 Mind the Mistake & 6.4 Patterns

When applying algebraic rules, it's very easy to misapply powers or forget the middle terms of an expansion.

Common Student Error:
\((5m + 6n)^2 = 25m^2 + 36n^2\)[cite: 22]

Correction: The middle term \(2ab\) was forgotten! The correct expansion is:
\((5m + 6n)^2 = 25m^2 + 60mn + 36n^2\)[cite: 22]

This Way or That Way: In mathematics, there are multiple ways of looking at a pattern, requiring creativity[cite: 22]. Even if expressions look different initially, applying identities proves they simplify to the same formula (e.g., \(k^2 + 2k\))[cite: 22].

Final Exercises (Page 154 - 156)

Q1. Compute \(46^2\) using Identity 1A for \((a + b)^2\)
Solution:
Step 1: Write 46 as \((40 + 6)\)[cite: 22].
Step 2: Expand using the identity: \((40 + 6)^2 = 40^2 + 2(40)(6) + 6^2\).
Step 3: Simplify: \(1600 + 480 + 36\)[cite: 22].
Final Answer: 2116[cite: 22]
Q2. Use a suitable identity to find the product: \((3a - 9b)(3a + 9b)\)
Solution:
Step 1: Recognize Identity 1C form: \((x - y)(x + y) = x^2 - y^2\).
Step 2: Substitute \(x = 3a\) and \(y = 9b\).
Step 3: Evaluate: \((3a)^2 - (9b)^2\).
Final Answer: \(9a^2 - 81b^2\)[cite: 22]
Q3. Which is larger? Find out without fully computing the product: \(14 \times 26\) or \(16 \times 24\).
Solution:
Step 1: Let \(a = 14 \times 26\) and \(b = 16 \times 24\)[cite: 22].
Step 2: Rewrite \(a\) using elements of \(b\): \(a = (16 - 2)(24 + 2)\)[cite: 22].
Step 3: Expand: \(16 \times 24 + 16 \times 2 - 2 \times 24 - 2 \times 2\). This equals \(b + (32 - 48 - 4) = b - 20\)[cite: 22].
Final Answer: \(16 \times 24\) is larger because \(a = b - 20\)[cite: 22].
